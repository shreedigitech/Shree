Flat Rate vs Reducing Balance

The same percentage rate can produce very different borrowing costs depending on how interest is calculated.

Reducing Balance

Interest is calculated on the outstanding principal.

As you repay principal, the outstanding balance falls and future interest generally becomes lower.

This method is commonly used for many bank and NBFC loans.

Flat / Fixed Rate

Interest is typically calculated using the original principal for the agreed period.

Therefore, a 10% flat rate is not equivalent to a 10% reducing-balance rate.

Always check your loan agreement for the actual calculation method.

What Is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular amount paid by a borrower toward a loan. An EMI generally contains two components: principal repayment and interest.

At the beginning of a typical reducing-balance loan, the interest component can represent a larger portion of the EMI because the outstanding principal is higher. As the principal is repaid, the interest component generally decreases.

EMI Calculation Formula

For a standard reducing-balance loan, the EMI formula is:

EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1)

Where P is the principal amount, r is the monthly interest rate and n is the number of monthly instalments.

What Is Reducing Balance Interest?

Under reducing-balance interest, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal. When part of the principal is repaid, the amount on which future interest is calculated becomes smaller.

What Is Flat Rate Interest?

Under a typical flat-rate calculation, the quoted interest rate is applied to the original principal rather than the declining outstanding balance.

This is why a flat interest rate and a reducing-balance interest rate with the same numerical percentage should not automatically be considered equivalent.

What Is Loan Prepayment?

Loan prepayment means paying an additional amount toward the outstanding principal before the scheduled maturity date.

For a reducing-balance loan, reducing the principal earlier can reduce future interest. Depending on the lender, a prepayment may reduce the EMI, reduce the remaining tenure, or be handled according to the specific terms of the loan agreement.

What Is Loan Foreclosure?

Loan foreclosure means completely closing a loan before its scheduled maturity. The borrower normally pays the eligible outstanding amount together with any applicable foreclosure, prepayment, tax or other contractual charges.

The foreclosure amount shown by this calculator is only an estimate. The actual amount should be obtained from your bank or NBFC because the lender may calculate interest and charges differently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is EMI?

EMI means Equated Monthly Instalment. It is the regular payment made toward a loan and generally consists of principal and interest.

What is the difference between flat and reducing interest?

Flat interest is typically calculated using the original principal, while reducing-balance interest is calculated on the outstanding principal. Therefore, identical percentages can result in different total interest costs.

Does prepayment reduce loan interest?

For a reducing-balance loan, early principal repayment can reduce future interest because interest is calculated on a lower outstanding balance.

Does keeping EMI unchanged after prepayment save more interest?

In many reducing-balance loans, keeping the EMI unchanged after prepayment can shorten the remaining tenure and may reduce future interest. The exact treatment depends on the lender's rules.

What is a loan amortization schedule?

An amortization schedule provides a month-by-month breakdown of opening balance, EMI, interest, principal repayment, prepayment and closing balance.

How is foreclosure amount calculated?

A simplified estimate is the eligible outstanding balance plus applicable foreclosure charges. Actual lender calculations may also include interest up to the closure date, taxes and other contractual charges.

Is a 4% foreclosure charge always applicable?

No. The foreclosure percentage is an input. Actual charges depend on the lender, loan product, agreement, timing and applicable rules.

Is this calculator accurate for every bank and NBFC?

The calculator provides mathematical estimates based on the values entered. Actual lender calculations may differ because of daily interest calculations, payment dates, rounding, fees, insurance, taxes and contractual conditions.
